# Sample BiasBreaker scenario

> Fictional learning example. Not legal advice or a candidate-evaluation tool.

## Scenario

Two candidates meet the core requirements for a client-facing program role.

- **Candidate A** has held the exact title at a well-known company.
- **Candidate B** has a different title at a smaller organization but demonstrates equivalent stakeholder management, program ownership, and measurable results.

An interviewer says: “Candidate A just feels safer. We know the training will be good.”

## Choose a response

**A.** Advance Candidate A because recognizable experience reduces risk.  
**B.** Ask both candidates the same evidence-based questions tied to the scorecard.  
**C.** Advance both without discussing the concern.  
**D.** Let AI rank the résumés to remove human subjectivity.

## Stronger response

**B. Ask both candidates the same evidence-based questions tied to the scorecard.**

## Bias pattern

This may reflect **pedigree bias** and **similarity or familiarity bias**. A recognizable employer or exact title can feel predictive even when it is not the job-relevant evidence the team agreed to assess.

## Evidence-based reframe

Instead of asking which background feels safer, ask:

- What specific outcome must this person deliver?
- What evidence has each candidate provided?
- Are we applying the same standard to both?
- Is the exact title truly required, or is it a proxy?
- What structured follow-up question would resolve the uncertainty?

## Reflection prompt

Where does your team use brand names, titles, schools, or “culture fit” as substitutes for direct evidence?
